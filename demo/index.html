Your AI agent needs to move your money. Don't give it your money.

An agent that manages a position needs to deposit and redeem on your behalf. The usual answer is to fund its wallet and hope. This one proves, on-chain, that the agent acts for a named human, within limits that human signed — while the agent's own balance stays at zero, permanently.

The cast

Four addresses, read from the chain just now. The one to watch is the agent: it is the only party actually sending the transactions, and it holds nothing before, during, or after.

It works

The agent deposited on the principal's behalf and redeemed the position back to them. Every transaction below was sent by the agent's key; every asset landed at the principal's address.

What it checks

Seven checks run before a single token moves, in this order, and each one has its own revert reason. No caller ever receives a bare selector or a library's error.

  1. Only the named agent may call. NotAgent
  2. The mandate is scoped to exactly one vault. WrongVault
  3. The mandate has not expired — enforced on the way out too. MandateExpired
  4. The principal is derived from the ERC-8004 registry, never taken from the mandate. AgentNotRegistered
  5. The recovered signer is that principal. InvalidSignature
  6. The principal has not revoked it. MandateRevoked
  7. The deposit fits the cumulative cap. ExceedsMandate

What it refuses

These are not screenshots. Each button sends a real eth_call to Ethereum Sepolia and prints the revert data the contract returns, decoded with its operands. The chain is answering, live, in your browser.

Seeded with one router address and a start block. The vault, the registry, the asset, the transaction hashes and the signed mandate itself are all read back off the chain — the mandate and its signature come out of the deposit transaction's own calldata. No dependencies, no build step, no server.
